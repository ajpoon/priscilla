This repository contains raw data, and codebase for plotting, derivation of motor angles, and Arduino motor control for both PRISCILLA Version 2 and Version 3 designs.

Abstract—
Mountain-dwelling bovids exhibit exceptional performance on rugged terrain, largely attributed to their specialized limb morphology. However, the individual contributions
of these traits remain insufficiently quantified. Previous studies have investigated joint compliance and limb-level mechanisms for terrain adaptability, while the role of
distal features such as toe splay remains less understood. This study examines how toe splay angle influences stability during inclined terrain traversal using a bio-inspired
quadrupedal robot. A parameterized two-toe foot with adjustable splay angles (0°, 30°, 60°) was tested on an inclined obstacle course (0–20°). Performance was evaluated using
success rate and traversal time under consistent morphology and gait conditions. Results show a strong positive relationship between toe splay angle and stability on steeper
inclines, while performance on lower inclines remained largely unchanged. Traversal time showed no statistically significant dependence on toe angle. These findings suggest that
increased toe splay enhances passive stability by enlarging the base of support and improving force distribution without additional control complexity. This aligns with biomechanical
observations of goat locomotion, where toe splay is primarily engaged on steep or uneven terrain, and extends prior work by isolating toe geometry as a key factor in slope performance.
Overall, toe splay emerges as a critical design parameter for improving stability in legged robots operating on uneven or inclined terrain.
